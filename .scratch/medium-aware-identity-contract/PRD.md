# Medium-Aware Identity Contract for Non-Photographic Output

## Problem Statement

CastMe's identity contract recognizes exactly one legitimate explanation for a deformed body: camera projection. That premise is correct for photography and is written into the contract as a medium-independent constant. When the requested output is a drawing — anime, chibi, illustration, hand-cut paper, or another non-photographic medium — four clauses misfire, and they misfire together because they share that one premise.

The Reference Transfer Avoid list rejects a structurally oversized head, compressed torso, miniature limbs, or doll-like anatomy that the selected projection does not explain. In a drawn medium the explanation is a drawing convention rather than a projection. OpenAI's own character-anchor guidance writes proportion instructions directly into the anchor prompt, so the current contract classifies the officially recommended practice as a defect.

The same list rejects age drift with no precondition. Head-to-body ratio encodes apparent age in Japanese illustration convention, so a strongly deformed figure necessarily reads younger than the person. The clause is self-contradicting in a drawn medium: the user's chosen proportion is itself the cause of the drift the clause forbids.

Identity Review passes only when no source-supported Protected Identity Cue shows material, unaccepted drift, and states that recognizability alone is insufficient. Protected Identity Cues is a fixed catalog that includes nose root, alar width, philtrum length, lip thickness, and skin texture. A strongly deformed drawing does not have those channels at all, so most catalog entries change materially, review fails, the single automatic correction fires, and the correction pulls the result back toward photography. That is the documented recipe for uneven stylization: fully converted eyes and hair on photographic skin and lighting. The contract manufactures the failure it is meant to catch.

Identity Prompt Constraints forbids waxy, plastic, and over-smoothed skin inside the general Face and identity item. Flat cel shading is over-smoothed skin by construction. The neighbouring Style-specific finish item already scopes itself to photorealistic requests; this item simply missed the same precondition.

None of these constraints is too strong. Each is correct for photography and wrong only because it is stated as a constant. The contract needs a medium dimension, not a relaxation.

## Solution

Introduce the **Medium Contract**: the resolved set of identity channels that the selected medium and deformation level can actually express. It is derived internally from the already-locked medium and requested deformation level, and from nothing else. It is not a new user-visible gate, a new workflow stage, or a new module.

Under a Medium Contract, Protected Identity Cues stop being one fixed catalog and become a channel matrix. Each channel resolves to one of three states. An **open** channel is one the medium draws as the person's own quantity, so it can still be compared cue by cue against the Primary Identity Anchor, and it is judged by Identity Review exactly as today. A **transferred** channel is one whose shape the medium's convention governs but which still carries recognition in place of channels the medium has closed — hairstyle outline, eye shape, distinctive marks, and characteristic wardrobe — and it is weighted up rather than merely retained. A **closed** channel does not exist in this medium, is excluded from drift judgment, and is disclosed to the user before generation.

Identity Review judges completion on open and transferred channels only. While the contract leaves even one structural channel open, the existing rule holds unchanged and recognizability remains insufficient on its own. Where it leaves none open, recognition through the transferred channels becomes the meaningful criterion, because nothing else remains to judge. That is the one place this specification lowers a bar, so it carries a concrete floor: the figure must stay identifiable in silhouette at thumbnail size, which is the standard readability test in character design and keeps the relaxed criterion checkable rather than a matter of impression. Review also gains one new failure mode that is independent of likeness: stylization that landed unevenly, with some features fully converted to the medium while others remain photographic.

Add a medium precondition to the three Avoid clauses rather than removing them. Deformation that the Medium Contract explains is locked intent; deformation that nothing explains remains a defect, and the photographic path is unchanged in every respect.

Disclose closed channels before generation, in plain visible language, only when the resolved contract closes channels that the photographic default would have protected. The disclosure sets expectations and gives Identity Review its comparison basis. It never enters the production prompt as a negative statement, because negative phrasing carries a documented risk of inducing the very feature it disclaims. When a closed channel must appear in the prompt at all, express it positively as what the drawing does instead.

## User Stories

1. As a user requesting an illustrated or anime version of myself, I want deliberate drawing proportions accepted as a valid medium convention, so that the style I asked for is not treated as an anatomy defect.
2. As a user requesting a strongly deformed drawn character, I want the enlarged head and small limbs treated as locked intent, so that the result is not silently corrected back toward camera-explainable anatomy.
3. As a user requesting a photographic portrait, I want oversized-head, compressed-torso, and doll-like anatomy still rejected, so that the medium dimension does not become a loophole for real anatomy errors.
4. As a user whose drawn request never asked for deformation, I want ordinary drawn proportions preserved, so that naming a drawn medium does not by itself authorize a deformed body.
5. As a user requesting a strongly deformed drawn character, I want the younger impression that the chosen proportion necessarily produces treated as part of that choice rather than as age drift, so that my own request is not classified as a failure.
6. As a user requesting an adult-proportioned drawn portrait, I want age drift still caught, so that the medium dimension does not license an unrequested change to how old I look.
7. As a user, I want the age impression that a drawn medium can still control expressed through concrete visible quantities such as cheek roundness, jaw length and sharpness, relative eye size, and upper-eyelid flatness, so that the constraint is checkable rather than an abstract instruction to keep my age.
8. As a user requesting flat cel shading, I want smooth unshaded skin accepted as the medium's surface language, so that the intended finish is not rejected as over-smoothed skin.
9. As a user requesting a photorealistic portrait, I want waxy, plastic, and over-smoothed skin still rejected, so that the precondition fix does not weaken photographic finish quality.
10. As a user receiving a heavily stylized drawn result, I want Identity Review to judge only the identity channels the medium can still express, so that review reaches a meaningful verdict instead of an automatic failure.
11. As a user receiving a drawn result, I do not want review to fail merely because a nose bridge, philtrum length, or skin texture is absent, so that the medium's normal simplifications are not counted as drift.
12. As a user receiving a drawn result, I do not want a failed review to trigger a correction that pulls my drawing back toward a photograph, so that I am not delivered a half-converted image.
13. As a user receiving a drawn result, I want uneven stylization checked as its own failure — eyes and hair fully converted while skin and lighting stay photographic — so that a mixed-medium image is not delivered as finished.
14. As a user whose drawn result came back half converted, I want it generated again with the medium stated for every surface, so that the repair finishes the conversion instead of patching the parts that stayed photographic.
15. As a user receiving an equal-proportion drawn portrait, I want the cues the medium still carries, such as nose bridge and upper-lip thickness, still judged, so that the channel matrix is not applied as one blanket exemption for everything drawn.
16. As a user receiving a photograph, I want the full Protected Identity Cue catalog applied unchanged, so that this feature changes nothing about photographic work.
17. As a user, I want the existing rule that recognizability alone is insufficient to keep applying wherever the medium still carries structural cues, so that the fix does not quietly lower the identity bar.
18. As a user receiving a strongly deformed drawn result where every structural channel has closed, I want recognition through the remaining channels treated as the meaningful criterion, so that review judges what the medium can actually deliver.
19. As a user requesting a strongly deformed drawn character, I want hairstyle outline, eye shape, distinctive marks, and characteristic wardrobe treated as the channels that now carry recognition, so that the result still reads as me after body differences are flattened.
20. As a user in that situation, I want those transferred channels weighted up rather than merely preserved, so that the drawing actively emphasizes what makes me identifiable.
21. As a user with a mole, scar, freckles, or under-eye shadow, I want those marks protected as recognition channels in a deformed drawn contract, so that the smallest details are not dropped as incidental.
22. As a user whose wardrobe becomes a recognition channel, I want it still owned by Styling, so that identity protection does not silently freeze the clothing choices I am allowed to change.
23. As a user receiving a strongly deformed drawn result, I want silhouette identifiability at thumbnail size required rather than merely preferred, so that the one place review relaxes its criterion still has a concrete floor.
24. As a user choosing a strongly deformed drawn output, I want to be told before generation which identity channels that choice closes, so that I can change my mind before spending a generation.
25. As a beginner, I want that disclosure in plain visible language, so that I am not shown a channel table or asked to understand facial geometry.
26. As a user whose request closes no channels, I do not want a disclosure at all, so that ordinary work does not gain a new notice.
27. As a user receiving the disclosure, I do not want it to become another question I must answer, so that the workflow does not gain a step.
28. As a user, I do not want a statement such as "this contract has closed the nose bridge" written into the production prompt, so that a negative phrasing does not risk inducing the feature it disclaims.
29. As a user, I want a closed channel expressed positively when it must appear in the prompt at all, such as describing the nose as simplified to a single mark rather than instructing the model not to draw a nose bridge.
30. As a user, I want the closed-channel record used for expectation setting and review comparison, so that it does useful work without entering the prompt.
31. As a user who accepted a deformed drawn contract, I do not want that acceptance to waive unrelated identity cues that remain open, so that one accepted transformation does not become a general likeness waiver.
32. As a user, I want the Medium Contract resolved from the direction I already locked, so that I am not asked a new question to obtain behavior that was already implied.
33. As a user requesting hand-cut paper, CG key art, or another non-photographic medium, I want the same medium dimension applied, so that the fix is not limited to one style I happened to name.
34. As a user requesting CG key art, I want identity-losing stylization still rejected, so that the medium dimension does not excuse a face that stopped being mine.
35. As a user receiving a drawn result, I want identity described as best effort without a similarity score or identity guarantee, so that the medium dimension does not come with a promise the skill cannot keep.
36. As a maintainer, I want the medium dimension added to existing constraints rather than the constraints relaxed, so that photographic strictness is preserved exactly.
37. As a maintainer, I want this implemented through the existing Identity Anchor owner and the existing non-photographic output recipe, so that no new module, agent, or workflow stage appears.
38. As a maintainer, I want Medium Contract recorded in the domain glossary with an accompanying ADR, because it changes the premise that projection is the only legitimate explanation for deformation.
39. As a maintainer, I want Uneven Stylization recorded in the domain glossary as a named result state but without an ADR, because the implementation has to judge it consistently, while its routing follows from the Medium Contract decision and is cheaply reversible.
40. As a maintainer, I want generic behavioral scenarios across several non-photographic media, so that the implementation cannot pass by recognizing one style name.
41. As a maintainer, I want the existing photographic scenarios preserved unchanged as controls, so that a regression in photographic review is caught immediately.
42. As a maintainer, I want no numeric proportion table, deformation-tier vocabulary, or style-axis catalog in this specification, so that this change stays limited to the contract conflict it exists to fix.

## Implementation Decisions

- Deepen the existing Identity Anchor owner and the existing Illustration or Other Non-Photographic Output recipe. Do not add a module, agent, generation stage, review stage, or user-visible gate.
- Add **Medium Contract** to the domain glossary and write an ADR for it. Unlike previous reversible refinements, this changes the premise of an existing decision — that camera projection is the only legitimate explanation for deformation — and it is not cheaply reversible once review behavior depends on it.
- Add **Uneven Stylization** to the domain glossary as a named result state alongside Structural Scale Failure, which it parallels. It is a completion failure with its own recovery route, and defining it only in prose would leave its judgment criterion unrecoverable from the glossary. It needs no ADR of its own: the routing follows from the Medium Contract decision and is cheaply reversible.
- Update the Protected Identity Cue glossary definition, which currently reads as medium-independent and stops being accurate once the catalog resolves per medium.
- Resolve the Medium Contract internally from the already-locked medium and any explicitly requested deformation, and from nothing else. Do not add a question, and do not infer a deformation level the user did not ask for. Scene conditions never participate: admitting lighting, framing, occlusion, or resolution would resolve a backlit or small-in-frame photographic portrait into a contract with no open structural channel, which is the loophole this specification exists to prevent, and those cases are already handled by source-supported evidence, Appearance Coverage, and the existing rule that states a review boundary when the face is too small to inspect.
- Resolve the contract once when the creative direction locks, record the resolved state of every cue, and have Identity Review read that record rather than resolve again. Re-resolve only when a Style Refresh or Scoped Option Refresh reopens the medium or the requested deformation, and disclose again when the closed set changes. One record serves the whole request including every regeneration, so a series cannot end up judged under two criteria. The disclosure already requires this resolution to exist before generation, so recording it adds no machinery, and it is what keeps the criterion decidable from the request rather than from the result.
- Replace the fixed Protected Identity Cue catalog with a channel matrix that resolves each channel to open, transferred, or closed under the current contract. Keep the existing catalog as the resolved matrix for photographic work so that the photographic path is bit-for-bit unchanged in behavior.
- Resolve a channel to open only when the medium draws that cue as the person's own quantity, so that it remains comparable cue by cue against the Primary Identity Anchor. A cue whose shape the medium's convention dictates is transferred even when it still tells one person from another, which is how a strongly deformed eye resolves; a cue the medium does not express at all is closed. Do not resolve by whether something is drawn in that place, because a flat-cel nose shadow is drawn and carries none of the person's own nose.
- Fix which cues the structural class contains, independently of medium, so that only a cue's state varies by request: face outline and bone structure, facial and forehead proportions, the brow-and-eye relationship, eye shape and size with eyelid structure and eye distance, the nose group, the lip and philtrum group, and chin and jawline. Exclude overall recognizability wherever the catalog states it, including recognizability across expressions, because it is the property the completion rule judges rather than a cue that rule can be conditioned on, and leaving it in makes the rule self-referential and never relaxable. Exclude eyebrow shape, whose geometric content the brow-and-eye relationship already carries. Exclude hairstyle and hairline together with distinctive visible styling details, which are the channels recognition transfers onto.
- Treat a transferred channel as an identity-review concern with increased weight, not as a change of ownership. Wardrobe, hair, and styling details that carry recognition remain owned by Styling and remain changeable by the user.
- Add a medium precondition to the oversized-head, age-drift, and waxy-or-over-smoothed-skin clauses. Model the wording on the Style-specific finish item, which already scopes itself to photorealistic requests within the same section.
- Keep every Avoid clause active for deformation, age change, or surface treatment that the resolved Medium Contract does not explain. The precondition is an explanation test, not an exemption for anything drawn.
- Judge Identity Review completion on open and transferred channels only. Preserve the existing rule that recognizability alone is insufficient while any structural channel remains open. Only where no structural channel is open does recognition through transferred channels become the completion criterion, because no other evidence exists. The condition is any rather than most, so that it is decidable without counting channels, which is out of scope.
- Require silhouette identifiability at thumbnail size whenever the resolved contract leaves no structural channel open. This is a hard completion requirement rather than a preference, because it is the only concrete floor under the one criterion this specification relaxes. Judge it as filled-shape readability, not as a score, and judge it after generation. It is the floor under the relaxed criterion and never an input to deciding whether the criterion relaxes; that decision was made from the channel record before the image existed.
- Add uneven stylization as an Identity Review failure mode independent of likeness: some features fully converted to the medium while others remain photographic. It is a visible, inspectable condition, unlike image degradation, and is therefore appropriate as a review item.
- Route an uneven-stylization failure to regeneration from the Primary Identity Anchor and locked brief. Never repair it with a surgical edit. The failure is that the medium instruction landed on some surfaces and not others, so the repair restates the medium's surface language for every visible surface — skin, hair, eyes, wardrobe, lighting, and background — rather than editing the surfaces that came out photographic. State positively what the medium renders; do not name the channels that came out photographic.
- Justify the no-edit rule by what an edit costs under this contract. Fine high-frequency detail collapses first under an edit, and distinctive marks are exactly the transferred channels a deformed contract leaves carrying recognition, so editing a strongly deformed drawn result degrades the only identity evidence still available. Where a result fails on both grounds, regenerate once rather than spending the single correction on a choice between them: regeneration repairs both, and the surgical edit is unavailable for the uneven-stylization half.
- Disclose closed channels before generation in beginner-facing visible language, only when the resolved contract closes channels the photographic default would have protected. The disclosure is a statement, not a gate.
- Never write a closed-channel statement into the production prompt. Negative phrasing carries a documented reverse-activation risk in diffusion models; the mechanism has not been verified on the active model, but the conservative handling costs nothing. Express a closed channel positively when it must appear in the prompt at all.
- Keep accepted risk scoped exactly as today. Accepting a deformed drawn contract accepts the channels that contract closes and nothing else.
- Keep identity best effort. Do not add a score, similarity measure, or guarantee, and do not describe a drawn result as verified likeness.
- Do not change safety handling, exact-text behavior, First-Pass Finish ownership, Scoped Option Refresh, quality and size behavior, or recovery-path selection beyond what the medium dimension requires.

## Testing Decisions

- Use `evals/golden-conversations.md` as the single behavioral seam. It is the existing highest seam and every prior specification in this repository uses it. Do not add a second harness.
- Assert externally visible behavior: which channels review judges, whether review passes or fails, whether a correction fires, what the disclosure says, and whether closed-channel statements appear in the production prompt. Do not assert exact prose, an internal matrix layout, or section-loading mechanics.
- Update the Shared checks entry that currently states Identity Review's completion rule as medium-independent. It must state that completion is judged on the channels the resolved Medium Contract leaves open, and that recognizability alone stays insufficient while any structural cue is among them.
- Preserve `Close crop to full-body proportion recovery` unchanged as the photographic control. It depends directly on the oversized-head clause this specification modifies and is the fastest signal that the precondition became a loophole.
- Preserve `Clean presentation does not pass Identity Review` unchanged. It is the scenario that encodes recognizability being insufficient, and its drift list includes skin texture, which a drawn contract would close. Add a drawn counterpart rather than editing it in place, so that the two sit side by side and the qualification is visible.
- Preserve `Clean cosplay execution cannot mask identity-wide drift`, `Identity Review reports visible cue drift only`, and `Identity preservation remains best effort` unchanged. Heavy styling on a photographic request must keep failing exactly as it does today.
- Preserve `Editorial, commercial, avatar, and illustration adapt impact`, `Final quality is shared across media`, and `Medium-adapted CG First-Pass Finish` unchanged. They must continue to pass without modification, proving the change is additive for the non-photographic media already covered.
- Add a strongly deformed drawn scenario in which structural channels close. Assert that review does not fail on absent nose, philtrum, or skin-texture channels, that no automatic correction fires, and that recognition is judged through hairstyle, eye shape, marks, and characteristic wardrobe.
- Add a case to that scenario in which the figure is recognizable at full size but its silhouette is unreadable at thumbnail size. Assert that review fails, proving the relaxed criterion has a floor.
- Add an equal-proportion drawn scenario in which structural channels remain open. Assert that nose bridge and upper-lip thickness are still judged, proving the matrix resolves per contract rather than exempting everything drawn.
- Add a switch-boundary scenario in which exactly one structural channel resolves to open, such as a drawn medium that still draws the person's own chin and jawline while convention governs the rest of the face. Assert that the existing rule applies in full, that recognizability alone does not pass, and that the silhouette floor does not apply. This is the only scenario that separates the any condition from a most condition; without it, an implementation that quietly counts channels passes both ends.
- Add an unexplained-deformation scenario in a drawn medium where the user requested no deformation. Assert that the oversized-head clause still fires, proving the precondition is an explanation test.
- Add an age scenario pairing a requested strong deformation, where the younger read is accepted, with an adult-proportioned drawn request, where age drift still fails.
- Add a cel-shading scenario asserting flat unshaded skin is accepted, paired with a photorealistic request where waxy and over-smoothed skin still fails.
- Add an uneven-stylization scenario in which eyes and hair are fully converted while skin and lighting remain photographic. Assert review fails on that ground even when likeness is intact, that the automatic correction regenerates from the Primary Identity Anchor and locked brief rather than editing the result, that the failed result is excluded from the image inputs, and that the regeneration prompt describes every visible surface in the medium's own language without naming which channels came out photographic.
- Add a disclosure scenario asserting that closed channels are stated before generation in visible language, that no gate or question is added, and that no closed-channel statement appears in the production prompt.
- Add a control scenario for a drawn request that closes no channels, asserting that no disclosure appears.
- Extend the existing `Accepted identity risk stays scoped` pattern with a drawn case, proving that accepting a deformed contract accepts only the channels that contract closes and does not waive unrelated open channels.
- Vary the medium across scenarios — at least one non-anime drawn medium — so that the implementation cannot pass by recognizing a single style name.
- Run the existing structural skill validator afterwards as a packaging and reference-integrity check, not as a second behavior seam.

## Out of Scope

- Deformation-tier vocabulary, head-to-body ratio values, tier presentation, and the tier Choice Gate. Those belong to the drawn-character capability specification that depends on this one.
- Style axes, stylization-intensity parameters, style-reference roles, and any style-name-to-visible-feature translation.
- Batch generation, multi-panel sheets, expression sets, and multi-angle reference packs.
- Multi-person images. Identity blending is a method-level limitation, and the skill remains single-anchor.
- Transparent background output and downstream cutout planning.
- Edit-chain depth, iterative degradation, and provenance-based narrowing of the automatic correction. Those are specified separately and are medium-independent. The recovery path for uneven stylization is not among them: that failure mode exists only under a requested medium conversion, so it is specified here.
- Any numeric threshold, channel count, similarity score, biometric matcher, or identity guarantee.
- A new module, agent, workflow stage, user-visible gate, persistent profile, or runtime component.
- Relaxing any constraint for photographic work.
- Style-specific or character-specific identity rules.

## Further Notes

- The following remain unverified and must not be written as settled fact. The reverse-activation mechanism was demonstrated on diffusion models with a separate negative-prompt field, and its transfer to constraints written inside an ordinary prompt is untested; the conservative handling is adopted because it costs nothing, not because the transfer is established. The claim that grading channels into a weighted budget improves likeness has no supporting evidence, only evidence of the reverse risk, so this specification takes only the part the evidence supports. The branch shape of the Medium Contract itself is a structural inference; this specification therefore states the outcomes it must produce rather than prescribing a mechanism.
- Channel transfer under strong deformation is drawn-industry consensus rather than a repository invention: flattening body differences and shifting recognition onto hairstyle, wardrobe, eye shape, and small marks is standard chibi practice.
- Background treatment is an independent axis from character rendering, with a dedicated role in Japanese animation production whose brief explicitly includes keeping backgrounds coherent with cel characters. That confirms the axis exists but does not validate any particular set of background options, which is why none appears here.
- Equal-proportion drawn portraits demonstrably still carry nose bridge and upper-lip thickness. Any implementation that closes structural channels for all drawn media has misread this specification.
- The framework already provides what the medium dimension needs: locked intent takes priority, conflicts route through a risk-choice gate rather than silent refusal, Inference Boundary is a mature explicit-authorization mechanism, and the non-photographic recipe already requires preserving identity through the medium's own shape and texture language. No module rewrite is required.
- The glossary entries and the ADR this specification requires are drafted in `domain-changes.md` alongside this file. They are staged rather than applied because canonical terms must not contradict runtime behavior, so they land with the implementation.
- Research backing this specification is in `.scratch/anime-stylized-identity/research.md`, which labels every design decision by evidence strength. Decisions marked as inference appear here only in this section or in Out of Scope.
